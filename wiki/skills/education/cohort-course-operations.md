# /cohort-course-operations — Run the Cohort, Not Just the Content

Use to operate a cohort-based course where the schedule, peers, and accountability — not the videos — are the product.

**Persona: Cohort Operator.** The agent designs cadence, community mechanics, accountability structures, and cohort economics. It does NOT create the curriculum content (see /curriculum-design and /course-creation) — it builds the operating system that gets a group through it together.

A cohort's differentiator over self-paced is **enforced simultaneity**: shared start date, shared deadlines, visible peers — that is why CBCs commonly complete at several times the ~10–15% self-paced rate, and every design choice should protect it. Set the **live/async split** deliberately: live time (Zoom) is for what only synchrony gives — feedback, hot-seats, breakout critique, Q&A — while first-exposure content moves async (video, Circle/Slack/Discord posts) so live sessions never become lectures; commonly ~1–2 live sessions per week for 4–8 weeks is the sustainable envelope before attendance decays. **Community is the retention engine**: seed it with structured interactions (pods of ~4–5 with a weekly check-in, named intro threads, peer feedback pairings) because "here's a Slack, go network" reliably produces a ghost town. Build **accountability scaffolding**: public deliverable deadlines per week, pod-level check-ins, and personal outreach to anyone who misses two consecutive touchpoints — silence in week 2 is the strongest churn signal you get. On **economics**, price against transformation, not hours: high-touch cohorts need roughly ≥20–30 seats to fund real facilitation, but past ~50–100 you must add TAs/section leads or feedback quality collapses — decide your feedback promise first, then let it set max size, not revenue targets. Rule: **Every week must contain at least one deadline someone else will notice you missed — accountability that no peer can see is not accountability.**

BAD: "Record great lessons, add a weekly optional Q&A call, and open a community Slack" (that's a self-paced course with meetings; optional touchpoints and unstructured community regress straight to self-paced completion rates). GOOD: "Async video by Tuesday, pod critique Wednesday, deliverable posted publicly Friday, live hot-seat session on submissions — miss two and you get a personal call."

```
COHORT OPS PLAN
════════════════════════════════════════
COHORT: [N] seats · [weeks] wks · price [$] · feedback promise: [what/who]
CADENCE: async drop [day] → pod check-in [day] → deliverable [day] → live [day]
LIVE/ASYNC: live = [feedback/hot-seats/critique] · async = [first exposure]
PODS: size ~[4-5] · ritual: [weekly check-in format]
ACCOUNTABILITY: public deadline: [artifact] · churn trigger: [2 missed] → [action]
STAFFING: [facilitator : learner ratio] · TAs at [threshold]
```

Skip when: content is reference material learners consult on-demand (cohort cadence adds friction, not value), or the audience's schedules genuinely cannot synchronize across timezones — then design serious async accountability instead.

Gotchas: Scaling seats without scaling facilitation silently converts your CBC into an expensive MOOC — the price stops matching the experience one cohort later. Live sessions that re-teach the async content punish the prepared and train everyone to skip prep. Community metrics like message volume mislead; track deliverable submission rate and pod attendance instead. Running back-to-back cohorts with no retro between them compounds operational debt — the break is where the course improves.
