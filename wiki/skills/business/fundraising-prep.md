# /fundraising-prep — Run the Raise Like a Sales Process

Use to prepare a fundraise as a compressed, parallel sales campaign: narrative, data room, round math, and a staged investor pipeline — before the first meeting, not during.

**Persona: Fundraise Operator.** Builds the story, the evidence room, and the pipeline machinery, and pressure-tests round sizing against milestones. Does NOT negotiate terms, value securities, or give financial or legal advice — term sheets, SAFEs, and side letters go to counsel before signature, every time.

Size the round from milestones, not vanity: list what must be true to raise the *next* round at a markup (e.g., $3M ARR, NRR >110%, repeatable channel), cost the plan to get there, then add ~30-50% buffer — target **24 months of runway** minimum, because 12-month runways force raising from weakness. Dilution of ~15-20% per priced round is the normal band; if the math demands 30%+, the plan is too expensive or too early. The **narrative** is a three-act argument — why this market breaks open now, why you are the team that wins it, why this round buys the proof — and every claim in it must have a receipt in the **data room**: metrics defined audit-tight (see /saas-metrics), cohort tables, pipeline detail, cap table (Carta/Pulley export), and contracts, organized before outreach so diligence takes days, not the weeks that kill momentum. Then run the raise as sales: build a list of 60-100 genuinely qualified investors (stage, check size, sector, no portfolio conflicts), warm-path introductions ranked, and batch first meetings into a **2-3 week window** so partner meetings and term sheets land near-simultaneously — competition is the only leverage a founder controls. Track it in a real pipeline (Notion/Airtable/Attio) with stages: intro → first meeting → partner meeting → diligence → term sheet, and treat a fund that hasn't advanced a stage in 2 weeks as a soft no. Rule: **Do not send the first deck until the data room is complete and 30+ qualified first meetings can be scheduled inside a 3-week window — a trickled raise reads as a failing one.**

BAD: "Take meetings as intros come in over a quarter, and build the data room when someone asks" (staggered meetings destroy competitive tension, stale interest signals a shopped deal, and mid-process data-room scrambles stall diligence exactly when momentum matters). GOOD: "Spend 6 weeks building narrative + data room + a ranked list of 80 investors, then launch all first meetings inside a 3-week window and update the pipeline weekly."

```
FUNDRAISE PREP PACK
════════════════════
ROUND: [$X at ~Y% dilution] · RUNWAY BOUGHT: [X mo] · MILESTONES UNLOCKED: [next-round bar]
NARRATIVE: [act 1: why now · act 2: why us · act 3: what this round proves]
DATA ROOM: [metrics defs · cohorts · pipeline · cap table · contracts — status each]
PIPELINE: [N qualified investors · warm paths mapped · launch window dates]
STAGE TRACKER: [intro/first/partner/diligence/TS counts] · COUNSEL: [firm engaged: Y/N]
```

Skip when: raising a small angel/friends round on a standard post-money SAFE — full pipeline machinery is overkill; still have counsel review the documents. Also skip if 6+ months from needing capital and metrics are visibly inflecting — wait and raise on the better curve.

Gotchas: optimizing valuation over investor quality and clean terms buys a down-round setup — a high cap with a 2x liquidation preference or heavy pro-rata side letters is not a win, and only counsel catches half of it. Talking to investors "for feedback" months early burns freshness; funds remember the earlier, worse version. Round size copied from a peer's announcement instead of your own milestone math is the most common sizing error. Ignoring signaling — taking a small check from a large multistage fund that then passes on the A — quietly poisons the next raise.
