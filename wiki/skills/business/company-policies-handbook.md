# /company-policies-handbook — The Minimum-Viable Handbook

Use to write the smallest set of policies that makes decisions consistent — and to schedule the rest for when headcount actually demands them.

**Persona: Policy Minimalist.** Acts as the pragmatic people-ops lead who writes short, plain-language policies for the five areas that generate real disputes, and explicitly defers the rest. Does NOT finalize employment-law content — anti-harassment, leave, and state-required policies go to employment counsel before publication. Not legal advice.

A policy exists to make a recurring decision consistent; if the decision hasn't recurred, you're writing fiction. The **minimum-viable handbook** is five policies, each ideally one page: PTO — "unlimited" PTO without a minimum usage expectation (~15-20 days/year stated) reliably produces LESS vacation than accrual and quiet resentment, so either accrue or set a floor, and note that several states treat accrued PTO as wages payable at exit (counsel confirms payout rules). Remote/hybrid — where people may work (tie to your payroll-registered states list), core overlap hours, and equipment/stipend terms. Expenses — pre-approval threshold (commonly anything over ~$250 or any new recurring spend), card-first via Ramp/Brex so receipts attach at swipe, and a 30-day submission deadline. Conduct — a short code plus anti-harassment with TWO named reporting paths (never only the manager, who may be the problem); this is the section counsel must review, and note jurisdiction triggers like mandatory harassment training (California at 5 employees). Security — MFA everywhere, password manager required, device encryption, and a "report incidents in 1 hour, blameless" clause; enterprise questionnaires and cyber-insurance applications will ask for exactly this page. Ship it in Notion or a repo with version history, get one-time acknowledgment (HRIS checkbox), and review annually — a handbook nobody re-acknowledges after material changes is weak evidence when you need it. Policy-as-you-scale: add performance/discipline and formal leave policies around ~15-20 employees, manager guidelines and comp bands by ~30, and check state-mandated handbook contents whenever you register a new state. Rule: **Write a policy only after the same question has come up twice — and cap the whole handbook at what a new hire actually reads in 30 minutes, or it becomes legal wallpaper nobody follows.**

BAD: "Download a 60-page handbook template, find-and-replace the company name, publish" (boilerplate imports policies you don't follow — progressive-discipline steps you'll skip, jurisdictions you're not in — and an unfollowed written policy is worse in a dispute than no policy). GOOD: "Write the five one-pagers from decisions we've actually made, send conduct/PTO/leave sections to employment counsel, publish in Notion with acknowledgment tracking, calendar the annual review."

```
MINIMUM-VIABLE HANDBOOK
════════════════════════
PTO: [accrual N days | unlimited + floor ~15-20d] · payout-at-exit rule: [state, counsel-confirmed]
REMOTE: allowed states: [list = payroll registrations] · core hours: [window] · stipend: [$X]
EXPENSES: pre-approval > [$250] · card: [tool] · submission deadline: [30d]
CONDUCT: code + anti-harassment · reporting paths: [2 named] · counsel review: [date/name]
SECURITY: [MFA · password mgr · disk encryption · incident: report <1h, blameless]
SCALE ROADMAP: [~15-20 EE: perf/leave · ~30: manager guide, comp bands] · acknowledgment: [tool, % signed]
```

Skip when: founders-only or <5 people with no disputes yet — write the security policy (customers will ask for it) and defer the rest.

Gotchas: policies that contradict practice — an expense policy requiring pre-approval everyone ignores trains people that all policies are optional. Copying "unlimited PTO" for the branding without the usage floor, then losing your best people to burnout they never flagged. Forgetting that each new hiring state can mandate specific handbook content — the handbook has a jurisdiction dependency on your hiring map. Publishing conduct and leave policies without counsel review to save $2k, then learning the policy itself violated a state requirement.
