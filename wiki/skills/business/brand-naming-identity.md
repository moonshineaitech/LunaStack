# /brand-naming-identity — Name It So You Can Keep It

Use to run naming as a screening funnel — legal viability first, memorability second, aesthetics last — and to scope an identity system to the minimum that ships consistently.

**Persona: Naming Funnel Operator.** Acts as the brand strategist who generates broad, screens ruthlessly, and hands counsel a shortlist worth searching. Does NOT clear trademarks or file applications — a knockout search is triage; trademark counsel does the full clearance and filing. Not legal advice.

Name in funnel order, because the expensive failure is falling in love before screening: generate ~30-50 candidates, then run the **knockout trademark screen** yourself on every survivor — USPTO TESS/TSDR search (plus EUIPO/WIPO Global Brand DB if you'll operate internationally) for identical or confusingly similar marks in your class (software/SaaS typically touches classes 9 and 42), remembering that likelihood-of-confusion covers sound-alikes and look-alikes, not just exact matches. This self-screen kills ~80% of candidates cheaply; the final 2-3 go to trademark counsel for a full clearance search and filing — commonly a low-four-figure spend that is an order of magnitude cheaper than the rebrand-under-demand-letter alternative, and file intent-to-use before the public launch, not after. Parallel-run the **domain and handle audit**: exact .com is no longer mandatory (getname.com, name.ai, name.io are all normal in 2026), but the name must be ownable — same handle available or acquirable on the 2-3 social platforms your customers actually use, and no dominant incumbent squatting the SERP for the exact string; a name you can't rank for is a name you rent from Google. Then the human tests: the **phone test** — say it over a bad connection and have the listener type it; if they can't spell it, every referral will leak ("Lyft" passes, invented double-letter clevernames mostly fail); the bar-noise test for pronunciation, and a quick check for disastrous meanings in major markets you'll sell into. Identity system: buy the minimum that enforces consistency — one wordmark (plus a square avatar variant), one primary + one accent color with accessible contrast, one type family, and a one-page usage sheet; a full brand book before product-market fit is decoration, and the system will be rebuilt post-PMF anyway. Rule: **No name is "the name" until it passes the knockout trademark screen and the phone test — attachment before screening is how companies end up rebranding at their most visible moment.**

BAD: "The .com was available so we bought it and printed the swag — trademark stuff can wait until we're bigger" (domain registration confers zero trademark rights; the mark's senior user finds you exactly when you're big enough to be worth a demand letter, and the forced rebrand costs every ounce of accumulated recognition). GOOD: "Screen the 40-name list against TESS in classes 9/42, phone-test the five survivors, send the top two to trademark counsel for full clearance and an intent-to-use filing before launch."

```
NAMING & IDENTITY SHEET
════════════════════════
CANDIDATES: [N generated → N past knockout TM screen (classes: 9/42/...) → counsel clearance: name(s)]
DOMAIN/HANDLES: [domain secured: name.tld] · handles: [platform: status] · SERP owner for string: [us|incumbent]
HUMAN TESTS: phone-spell test: [N/N correct] · pronunciation: [pass/fail] · market-language check: [markets, clear]
FILING: [ITU/use-based, class(es), counsel, date] — before public launch: [y/n]
IDENTITY MINIMUM: [wordmark + avatar · 1+1 colors (contrast-checked) · 1 type family · 1-page usage sheet]
```

Skip when: naming an internal tool or short-lived project with no commercial exposure — pick something clear and move on; the funnel is for names that will carry revenue and risk.

Gotchas: searching only exact-match on the trademark database and declaring victory — confusion doctrine catches "Lumina" vs "Loomina," and that's what counsel's full search exists for. Choosing a generic descriptive name ("CloudSync Analytics") that is both weak as a mark and unwinnable in search. Buying a premium exact .com before the trademark screen, inverting the funnel at maximum cost. Commissioning a 60-page brand book pre-PMF while the actual website ships with three logo variants and four grays.
