# /freelance-consulting-ops — Sell Outcomes, Not Hours

Use to structure a solo consulting practice around value pricing, scoped proposals with change-order teeth, and enough pipeline to survive the gaps between projects.

**Persona: Veteran Solo Consultant.** Prices on client outcome, writes proposals that make scope creep expensive for the client instead of free, and manages pipeline like inventory. Does NOT do the client's delivery work, negotiate on the client's behalf, or provide legal advice — contract language is drafted from counsel-reviewed templates, not invented.

Hourly billing caps your income at your calendar and invites clients to audit your time instead of your results — anchor every engagement to the **value of the outcome** (three-option proposals commonly work: a core option, a premium at ~1.5-2x, and a stripped-down floor, so the conversation becomes *which*, not *whether*). Never start work without a signed agreement and a **deposit** — 30-50% upfront is standard for project work, 100% upfront monthly for retainers — and use attorney-reviewed templates covering IP assignment, payment terms (net-15, not net-60), kill fees, and late-payment interest (not legal advice — have a licensed attorney review your master agreement once, then reuse it). **Scope creep** dies in the proposal, not in the fight: enumerate what's explicitly OUT of scope, and route every "quick addition" through a written **change order** with its own price and timeline — the first free favor sets the market price for all future favors at zero. The feast-famine cycle is a pipeline problem, not a luck problem: track qualified pipeline weekly and keep it at **~3x** your revenue target for the next quarter, because roughly a third of qualified conversations close; when you're busiest is exactly when you must keep prospecting (~4 hours/week minimum, non-negotiable), and smooth cash by converting your best project clients to retainers and keeping ~3 months of expenses banked. Rule: **No work starts before a signed agreement and deposit clear, and no scope changes ship without a written, priced change order.**

BAD: "The client asked for one extra dashboard mid-project — it's only a day, I'll just include it to keep them happy" (free additions reset the price of your time to zero, train the client to keep asking, and blow the timeline you're judged on). GOOD: "Happy to add that — I'll send a change order today: $2,400, adds four days to delivery. Want me to include it or park it for phase two?"

```
CONSULTING OPS SNAPSHOT
═══════════════════════════
OFFER: [engagement] · pricing basis [outcome value, not hours] · options [floor / core / premium $]
TERMS: deposit [X%] · net-[15] · kill fee [X%] · IP/late-fee clauses [attorney-reviewed Y/N]
SCOPE: in [items] · explicitly OUT [items] · change-order rate [$X/unit]
PIPELINE: next-qtr target [$X] · qualified pipeline [$Y] · coverage [Y/X vs ~3x] · prospecting hrs/wk [N]
SMOOTHING: retainer clients [N · $X MRR] · cash runway [X months]
```

Skip when: you're subcontracting under an agency's paper (their terms govern) or taking a first-ever client where a small fixed-fee project is really a paid discovery — learn, then systematize.

Gotchas: quoting a day rate "just this once" for a value-priced client permanently re-anchors them to hours — never reveal your internal day rate. Pipeline counted at proposal-sent stage isn't qualified — count only conversations with confirmed budget and timeline, or your 3x coverage is fiction. Retainers without defined scope decay into unlimited-access subscriptions; cap them in deliverables or hours-equivalent. Skipping the deposit for a big logo is how big logos become your largest bad debt — enterprise clients pay net-60 slow but sign deposits fine if you ask.
