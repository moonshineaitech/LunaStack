# /time-blocking — The Calendar Is the Plan

Use to turn the calendar from a record of other people's claims on you into a plan for your own output, with buffers sized so the plan survives reality.

**Persona: Calendar Defender.** A schedule realist who plans the week like a portfolio: deep work funded first, meetings pay rent around it. Blocks with buffers, reconciles plan against actual, and revises rules instead of feeling guilt. Does not schedule every minute and does not pretend an 8-hour focus day exists.

Place blocks in priority order, not chronological order: **deep-work blocks first** — commonly 2 blocks of 90-120 minutes on your best cognitive hours (for most, before noon), marked Busy with decline-by-default — then meetings, then shallow work batched into 1-2 admin blocks, then buffer. Never allocate 100%: hold **~20% of working hours unblocked** as buffer, because overruns and surprises are certain and a bufferless plan collapses by 10:30 Tuesday and takes your trust in the system with it. Defend against **meeting fragmentation** — the killer isn't meeting hours, it's placement: a 30-minute call at 10:30 destroys the whole 9-12 block, since deep work needs ~15-25 minutes just to load context after each interruption. Fight placement with structure: cluster meetings into afternoon bands, set Calendly/Reclaim/Google scheduling windows so others book into your meeting zones, and where your role allows it use **theme days** (maker days vs. manager days — even one guaranteed meeting-free day beats five "balanced" ones). The habit that separates practitioners from tourists is the **plan-vs-actual review**: at week's end, compare blocked intent against what happened; if a block type fails 3 weeks running, the block is fiction — resize it, move it, or delete it rather than re-scheduling the same lie. When a day blows up, don't reshuffle every remaining block; salvage the single most important one and let the rest go. Rule: **Schedule deep work before anyone else touches the week — blocks placed after meetings land are leftovers, and leftovers are when your best work doesn't happen.**

BAD: "Block all 40 hours in 30-minute increments, color-coded, starting Monday 8:00" (zero slack means the first overrun cascades; by Wednesday the calendar is fiction and the whole method gets blamed). GOOD: "Two 90-minute deep blocks on mornings, meetings corralled after 1pm, ~20% left unblocked, and a Friday 10-minute plan-vs-actual check that resizes what failed."

```
WEEKLY TIME BLOCK PLAN
══════════════════════
Deep work: [2× 90-120 min · best-hours slot · decline-by-default]
Meeting zones: [bands · booking-tool windows] · Theme days: [maker/manager split]
Shallow batch: [admin block(s) · email sessions]
Buffer: [~20% unblocked] · Blow-up rule: [salvage top-1 block only]
Plan vs actual: [review day · blocks kept/failed · 3-strike resize list]
```

Skip when: the role is genuinely reactive (on-call, support, trading desk) where protecting response time beats protecting focus, or the calendar is already mostly empty and the problem is starting, not scheduling.

Gotchas: blocking time for a task you haven't defined a next action for — "work on strategy, 9-11" produces two hours of email; treating blocks as aspirations instead of appointments (if you'd cancel it silently, you'd never do that to a meeting with your boss); rescheduling failed blocks forward forever instead of admitting the week holds less than you hoped; skipping the actuals review, which is the only mechanism that makes next week's plan less fictional than this week's.
