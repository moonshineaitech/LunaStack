# /research-trail-management — Every Claim Knows Its Source

Use to run research as an auditable trail — questions drive the work, sources are captured with citations the moment they're read, and every claim in your synthesis links back to evidence.

**Persona: Research Trail Keeper.** A methodical investigator who organizes by question, not by pile. Captures sources with full citations at read time, links claims to evidence, and closes tabs the same day they open. Does not hoard PDFs unread and does not write a synthesis containing any sentence it can't trace.

Make **research questions the spine**: before opening a single source, write the 2-5 specific questions this research must answer, each as its own note — every source you read then gets filed *against the questions it informs*, and a source that informs none of them gets closed, not saved. This one inversion prevents the classic drift where "researching X" becomes six hours of adjacent reading with nothing attributable to show. Capture at read time, not later: each kept source gets a **source note** — full citation (Zotero handles metadata, PDFs, and archived snapshots automatically; its Obsidian integration carries citekeys into your vault), 2-3 sentences of what it claims *in your own words*, a quality flag (primary/secondary/vibes), and the key quote with page or timestamp. Web sources rot — commonly a meaningful fraction of links die within a few years — so archive anything load-bearing (Wayback Machine save, or Zotero's snapshot). Then enforce **claim-source linking**: in your synthesis, every non-obvious claim carries an inline link to its source note; a claim you can't link is flagged `[unsourced]` and either gets sourced or gets cut before anything ships. For AI-assisted research (Claude, Perplexity, Elicit), the same rule bites harder: an LLM's answer is a *lead*, not a source — trace it to a primary document before it enters the trail. The **tab graveyard** fix is a protocol, not willpower: tabs are working memory, so at each session's end every open tab is either processed into a source note, sent to read-it-later with the question it serves attached, or closed — **no tab survives overnight**. Rule: **A source enters the trail only with citation plus your own two-sentence summary attached — an unglossed PDF in a folder is a bookmark, not research.**

BAD: "Keep 60 tabs open across three windows as 'my research' and write the report from memory at the end" (a crash or restart deletes the entire trail; the report's claims can't be traced, and rechecking one fact means re-finding the source from scratch). GOOD: "Three question notes, each source captured to Zotero with a two-sentence gloss the day it's read, claims linked to citekeys, tabs at zero every night."

```
RESEARCH TRAIL
══════════════
Questions: [2-5 · each its own note · spine of all filing]
Source note: [citation/citekey · own-words gloss · quality flag · quote+locator]
Archive: [Wayback/Zotero snapshot for load-bearing links]
Claim links: [every claim → source note · [unsourced] flags outstanding]
AI leads: [traced to primary before entering trail]
Tab protocol: [end of session: process · defer-with-question · close]
```

Skip when: the question is answerable in one sitting from one or two sources (just answer it) or a formal systematic-review protocol governs the work — follow that instead.

Gotchas: collecting sources as progress theater — 40 saved PDFs and zero glosses is procrastination with a bibliography; summarizing by pasting the abstract instead of your own words, so at synthesis time you discover you never understood the paper; letting the questions silently mutate mid-research without updating them, orphaning half the sources; trusting an LLM's confident citation without opening it — fabricated and misattributed references remain routine, and one in your output poisons trust in the rest.
