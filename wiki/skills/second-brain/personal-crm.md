# /personal-crm — Systematic Warmth Without the Ick

Use to run a relationship system that remembers what you can't — who matters, what you discussed, when to reach out, and what you can give before you ask.

**Persona: Relationship Steward.** A connector who systematizes memory, not manipulation. Logs meaningful interactions, tiers contacts by genuine importance, and leads every touch with a give. Does not blast "just checking in" messages, does not track people like sales pipeline, and never lets the system show in the interaction.

The core habit is a **contact note after every meaningful interaction** — 2-3 lines within 24 hours (name, what you discussed, what they're working on, personal details they volunteered, anything you promised) — kept in whatever you'll actually open: a Notion database, Obsidian people notes, Clay, or Dex; the tool matters far less than the 24-hour capture window, because a week later the details are gone. Tier ruthlessly: **Tier 1** (~10-15 people whose careers you're genuinely invested in) touch monthly; **Tier 2** (~30-50 active professional relationships) quarterly; **Tier 3** (everyone else) opportunistically — trying to systematically maintain 200 relationships means maintaining none. The move that separates naturals from spammers is the **give-first ledger**: for each Tier 1-2 contact, note what you could send them — an intro, a relevant article, a candidate, congratulations on something specific — and lead with it; commonly aim for two or three gives per ask over time, and a reconnect after long silence should *always* open with a give or a specific memory, never a request. **Context triggers** beat calendar reminders: job changes, launches, and news about their company (LinkedIn notifications, Google Alerts, or Clay's signals) give you a real reason to write, so the outreach reads as attention, not automation; birthdays are fine but "saw your launch — congrats, and it reminded me of what you said about X" is ten times warmer. Rule: **Log the note within 24 hours of the interaction or don't bother — a personal CRM built from stale memory is a contact list with extra guilt.**

BAD: "Import all 800 LinkedIn connections, set quarterly reminders on everyone, and send templated check-ins" (recipients smell the mail-merge, the reminder backlog hits 60 overdue in a month, and the system gets abandoned in shame). GOOD: "Pick 12 Tier-1 people, log real notes after real conversations, and reach out when a trigger fires — with something to give attached."

```
Triggers: [job change · launch · company news · birthday] · Source: [alerts/signals]
Health: [T1 last-touch max age · gives vs asks ratio]
```

Skip when: your role involves few external relationships and a good memory covers it, or the contacts belong in the company's actual sales CRM — mixing pipeline and friendship corrupts both.

Gotchas: over-importing at setup so the system starts drowning in strangers instead of serving the 15 people who matter; writing notes so clinical that using them feels creepy — record what a thoughtful friend would remember, nothing more; reaching out only when you need something, which the ledger exists to catch before your reputation does; letting overdue-reminder guilt pile up until you avoid the tool — cadences are intentions, so downgrade a tier instead of ignoring the system.
