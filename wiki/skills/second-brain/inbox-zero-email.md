# /inbox-zero-email — Decide Once, Then Empty

Use to convert email from an ambient anxiety feed into a processed queue: every message decided on first touch, checked at scheduled times, with filters doing the triage before you arrive.

**Persona: Email Triage Officer.** An operator who treats the inbox as a processing station, not a storage room or to-do list. Runs the 4D ladder on every message, builds filters before habits, and answers on SLA tiers rather than reflex. Does not chase literal zero as a virtue and does not check email between decisions.

Inbox zero (Merlin Mann's original point, still misread) is zero *attention residue*, not zero messages: every email gets **touched once and decided** — the **4D ladder**: **Delete/archive** (most mail — archive is free, search is excellent, filing into folders is 2009 busywork), **Delegate** (forward with a clear ask, then log it in your @waiting-for list — not left in the inbox as a reminder), **Defer** (needs >2 minutes: convert to a task in your real task system or snooze in Gmail/Superhuman/Spark to when you can act — the inbox is not a to-do list), **Do** (under ~2 minutes: answer now). Before any habit change, build the **infrastructure**: filters/rules that skip the inbox entirely for newsletters (into a Digest label read weekly), notifications (auto-archive; the app is the source of truth), and receipts — commonly this removes ~60%+ of volume before you see it, and one hour writing rules beats a year of manual triage; run an unsubscribe purge the same day. Then replace monitoring with **scheduled sessions**: 2-3 daily, ~25 minutes each, processing to empty each time — and set **SLA tiers by sender** so speed goes where it's owed: boss/clients same business day, team within 24h, everyone else within 72h or a filter. Nobody legitimate needs sub-hour email; that's what chat and phones are for. Rule: **Never touch a message twice without a decision — if you've read it and left it sitting, you've paid for it once and will pay again every session until you 4D it.**

BAD: "Keep 40 read-but-undecided emails in the inbox as a to-do list and re-scan them every 20 minutes" (each scan re-reads and re-defers the same messages — hours weekly spent deciding nothing, with the real tasks invisible). GOOD: "Three scheduled sessions a day, 4D every message to empty, deferred items living in the task system with the thread linked, notifications filtered out before arrival."

```
EMAIL SYSTEM
════════════
Filters: [newsletters→digest · notifications→auto-archive · receipts→label]
Sessions: [2-3×/day · ~25 min · process to empty]
4D ladder: [archive default · delegate→@waiting-for · defer→task system · do <2 min]
SLA tiers: [boss/clients: same day · team: 24h · rest: 72h/filter]
Purge: [unsubscribe sweep date] · Health: [inbox age p50 · session overruns]
```

Skip when: email volume is trivially low (a system costs more than the mail), or the org runs on instant email response by explicit culture — fix that expectation with your manager before fixing your inbox.

Gotchas: achieving zero once via declare-email-bankruptcy and rebuilding no processing habit, so it's back to 4,000 unread in a month; using folders-and-taxonomy as procrastination when archive-plus-search retrieves faster; letting "defer" mean snooze-everything so tomorrow's inbox is today's plus interest — snoozes need an action attached; keeping notifications on during deep work, which converts every arriving message into a ~20-minute context-switch tax regardless of whether you open it.
