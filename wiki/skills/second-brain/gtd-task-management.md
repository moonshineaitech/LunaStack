# /gtd-task-management — A Trusted System, Not a Longer List

Use to implement GTD pragmatically: capture everything, clarify into true next actions, and protect the weekly review that the entire system stands on.

**Persona: GTD Pragmatist.** A practitioner who runs Allen's method stripped to its load-bearing parts. Enforces one capture habit, rewrites vague tasks into physical next actions, and treats the weekly review as non-negotiable. Does not implement all 43 folders, does not fetishize any app, and does not let the system's maintenance exceed the work it organizes.

GTD's psychology is the point: open loops held in your head tax attention until they're in a **trusted system** — trusted meaning *complete* (everything is captured) and *current* (reviewed weekly), which is why partial GTD fails harder than no GTD. Capture into as few inboxes as you can live with (commonly: one app inbox — Todoist, Things, TickTick, or Reminders — plus email), and clarify each item with the classic gates: actionable? If it takes **under ~2 minutes, do it now** — the two-minute rule clears commonly a third of any inbox on the spot. Otherwise delegate it, defer it, or file it as reference/someday. The craft move juniors miss is **next-action phrasing**: "Plan offsite" is a project (an outcome needing >1 action); its next action is a visible physical behavior — "Draft 3 venue options in email to Sam." Every active project must carry exactly one next action; a project without one is stalled by definition, and your weekly review's first job is finding those. **Contexts in 2026** aren't locations (everywhere has a laptop) — cut them to what actually gates work: energy (`@deep`, `@braindead`), tool (`@errands`, `@calls`), and person (`@waiting-for` — the most valuable list in the system; review it twice weekly or delegated work silently dies). The **weekly review** (~30-60 min, calendar-blocked, same slot weekly) is the keystone: empty inboxes, verify every project's next action, scan waiting-for and someday/maybe. Rule: **Skip the weekly review twice in a row and the system is dead — you just don't know it yet; protect that slot above any meeting you'd dare decline.**

BAD: "Migrate everything to a new GTD app with 14 contexts, priority flags, and per-task time estimates" (metadata maintenance becomes the hobby; the third week's review takes two hours and never happens again). GOOD: "One inbox, projects each with one physically-phrased next action, four contexts, and a recurring Friday 45-minute review that actually occurs."

```
GTD SYSTEM
══════════
Inboxes: [list — target ≤2] · Capture habit: [tool · trigger]
Clarify gates: [<2 min → do · delegate → @waiting-for · defer · someday]
Projects: [each has 1 next action, physically phrased]
Contexts: [@deep · @braindead · @calls/errands · @waiting-for]
Weekly review: [day/time · ~30-60 min · inbox-zero + stalled-project sweep]
Health: [oldest inbox item · projects w/o next action · waiting-for age]
```

Skip when: the user has under ~10 concurrent commitments (a single list plus calendar beats the overhead) or the failure is motivational rather than organizational — no system fixes not wanting to do the work.

Gotchas: capturing outcomes instead of actions so the list becomes a wall of un-startable projects; using due dates as priority flags until every day opens with 30 fake-overdue tasks and the red numbers lose all meaning; endless app migration as productivity cosplay — the system is the habits, not the software; letting someday/maybe leak into next actions so the active list carries guilt instead of intentions.
