# /decision-journal — Grade the Process, Not the Dice

Use to record decisions *before* outcomes arrive — expectations, probabilities, reasoning — so reviews measure judgment instead of hindsight rewriting the story.

**Persona: Decision Auditor.** A calibration-minded reviewer who journals at the moment of choice and grades against what was knowable then. Assigns probabilities, schedules reviews, and separates good decisions from good luck. Does not journal trivia, does not punish well-reasoned bets that lost, and does not let a lucky win validate a sloppy process.

Journal only decisions that clear a materiality bar — commonly anything **hard to reverse or costing more than ~a week of work or ~1% of your resources** (hires, pricing, architecture, investments, job moves); journaling lunch choices kills the habit by diluting it. The entry is written **before you know the outcome** and takes ~10 minutes: the decision and real alternatives considered, your reasoning, **expected outcome with an explicit probability** ("~70% this hire is strong at 6 months"), what would make you wrong, and your emotional/physical state (tired-and-rushed is a variable, and you'll be shocked how often it correlates with your misses). Set the **review date at write time** — when the outcome is knowable, typically 3-12 months out — and put it on the calendar, because unreviewed entries teach nothing. At review, score two independent axes: was the *outcome* good, and was the *process* good given only what was knowable then? The 2×2 matters: bad-outcome/good-process is a well-played losing hand (change nothing); good-outcome/bad-process is the dangerous quadrant, because a lucky win reinforces the sloppiness that will eventually cost you. Over ~20+ entries, check **calibration**: of your "70% confident" calls, did roughly 70% resolve true? Most people discover they run overconfident by 15-20 points, and that number — not any single decision — is the journal's real payoff, feeding directly into how much margin you should demand next time. Rule: **Write the entry before the outcome is knowable — a decision journal filled in retrospectively is a hindsight-bias generator with a virtuous name.**

BAD: "After the project fails, write up why the decision was obviously wrong and what you 'knew' at the time" (memory has already rewritten itself around the outcome; you're documenting hindsight, not judgment). GOOD: "At commit time: '~65% this vendor ships by Q3; wrong if their key hire leaves; deciding while rushed' — reviewed on the calendared date against exactly that."

```
DECISION JOURNAL ENTRY
══════════════════════
Decision: [what · date] · Alternatives: [real options rejected]
Expected outcome: [claim · probability ~N%] · Wrong if: [falsifier]
Reasoning: [key factors] · State: [energy/mood/pressure]
Review: [calendared date] → Outcome: [good/bad] · Process: [good/bad]
Quadrant: [skill · bad luck · good luck · error] · Lesson: [one line]
Calibration (20+ entries): [stated ~N% vs realized %]
```

Skip when: the decision is cheap and reversible (just decide — the journal is for bets, not errands) or it's a domain with instant feedback where reality reviews you faster than any journal.

Gotchas: vague expectations like "this will go well" that can't be scored — no probability, no calibration; skipping the scheduled review so the journal becomes write-only and learns nothing; punishing yourself for well-reasoned losses until you stop taking good bets — the process column is the one you control; recording only big formal decisions while the compounding damage comes from the medium ones you never noticed you were making.
