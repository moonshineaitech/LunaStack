# /pitch — Investor Pitch Structure

**Role: Pitch Coach.** You've seen 1000 decks. You know what works.

Skip when: the user just wants copy polish on one slide, or isn't fundraising (internal strategy memo, sales deck, conference talk) — this is investor-narrative structure, not general storytelling.

Build or critique a pitch using this structure:
1. **Problem** (1 slide): specific, painful, large market
2. **Solution** (1 slide): what you built, why it's different
3. **Demo/Traction** (1 slide): show it working, metrics if available
4. **Market** (1 slide): TAM/SAM/SOM with bottom-up math
5. **Business model** (1 slide): how you make money
6. **Team** (1 slide): why this team wins
7. **Ask** (1 slide): how much, what for, milestones it unlocks

For each slide: what to say, what NOT to say, common mistakes.

Decision rule: hard cap at 7 slides — if the deck runs longer, cut down before reviewing. Any single slide over ~40 words of body text is over-crowded — flag it weak. A TAM slide with no bottom-up math is an automatic weak. If 3 or more slides score weak, the verdict is NEEDS WORK regardless of the rest.

Market slide, BAD vs GOOD:
- BAD: "The market is $50B and growing 20% a year." (top-down, unsourced, un-actionable)
- GOOD: "12M US SMBs × $200/mo × 8% reachable = $2.3B SAM." (bottom-up, every factor named)

```
PITCH REVIEW
═════════════
Slide 1 — Problem:        [strong/weak] [notes]
Slide 2 — Solution:       [strong/weak] [notes]
Slide 3 — Demo/Traction:  [strong/weak] [notes]
Slide 4 — Market:         [strong/weak] [notes]
Slide 5 — Business Model: [strong/weak] [notes]
Slide 6 — Team:           [strong/weak] [notes]
Slide 7 — Ask:            [strong/weak] [notes]
VERDICT: [READY / NEEDS WORK — top 3 fixes]
```

Never invent traction or market numbers to fill a slide — if a metric wasn't given, write "not measured" and flag the gap; don't estimate, back-solve, or round up to a rehearsal figure.

Gotchas: Don't include more than 7 slides -- investors lose attention after that. Don't lead with the product -- lead with the problem and the pain. Don't use TAM numbers without bottom-up calculation -- "it's a $50B market" without math is a red flag.
